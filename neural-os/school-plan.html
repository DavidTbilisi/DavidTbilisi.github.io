II   The Curriculum

Why Cambridge is the bridge.

A curriculum is the one thing that survives every move. Choose the right one once and a child can change countries without changing what they study, what grade they sit in, or which exams they take. Three were on the table.

Chosen

Cambridge

The English national system, exported. IGCSE, then A-Levels.

  • Thousands of schools across 160+ countries
  • Native to the UK; accepted across the EU
  • Subject-by-subject — mid-stage transfer is routine
Runner-up

IB

International Baccalaureate. Broader and more demanding.

  • Fewer schools, especially in smaller cities
  • Whole-profile Diploma — harder to part-transfer
  • Prestigious, but a thinner network

Free and native — but it stops at the border.

  • No automatic recognition abroad

III   The Inner Curriculum

Load the hardest things first.

Section II chose the curriculum that travels across borders. This one chooses what to load into it first — the capacities that travel across futures. The same move, aimed inward: keep every door open by front-loading the things that are nearly impossible to add later. That is what “opened to any opportunity” actually costs — and, crucially, when the bill comes due.

The Asymmetry
Hard can always be walked down to easy. Easy rarely climbs back up to hard. Teaching the harder thing keeps both branches; teaching the easy one quietly closes one.
The Window
Plasticity is an asset that depreciates. An ear for language, for music, for abstraction — cheap to buy young, dear or barred to buy late. Spend the cheap years on what is only cheap once.
Compounding
Maths, a hard language, deep reading — the substrate the rest is built on. Loaded early, every later subject costs less. Loaded late, each one charges interest.

Hardest-first is not harshest-first. Met early enough and in play, a proof or a third tongue is never felt as “hard” — it is simply the water she swims in. Difficulty is mostly a property of late arrival; front-loading removes the fear, not only the gap.

Her hard-first spine — in your words

One spine, four faces: mathematics as the medium, logic and formal reasoning as the method, problem-solving as the proof it took hold. Taught first, taught early, taught as play — before she can learn to call it hard.

Every later door — science, code, law, money, the arguments she will one day need to win — opens cheaper once this one is hers. So it is the floor we lay before anything else is allowed to stand on it.

And one that can't wait.   The spine compounds forever, and could be added late if it had to. A second class of skill cannot: the ear for music, the ear for a new tongue, the body's fluency on an instrument — cheap to buy now, dear or barred to buy at twenty-five. At almost seven she is right on the cliff, so the order goes to whatever is still un-banked: pitch shuts first and hardest, while the three tongues she already lives in are largely won and now need locking, not catching. The spine for what compounds; the window-skills, ranked, for what expires. Lay the spine early because it pays forever; lay these early because there is no later.
The window-skills, in the order their doors shut
Now

Music & pitch

The hardest, soonest cliff — pitch closes around seven, and it is the one major window she has most likely not yet banked. If she is to have a musician's ear, the decision is essentially this year, not a later one.

Lock

Language → literacy

Her ear for the tongues she already lives in is largely won by immersion. The live work now is keeping Georgian and Russian from eroding on the move and driving all three to full reading and writing — and adding a fourth now if ever, while a near-native entry is still open.

Slack

One sport

A softer window with no hard stop at seven. Start it this year for the body, the nerve, and the attention every hour of the spine quietly draws on — but it is the one you can stage without losing it.

Keeping it — the maintenance dose

The spineMath · logic · reasoning Most-days mixed retrieval — a few problems from memory, topics interleaved, old ones spiralled back in. Drilled daily while young; later held by a weekly spiral review. Fluent speed. It rusts to slow-but-correct long before it is truly lost — reflex slides back to effort.

In the lab's terms.   This is just the maintenance tail of your own knowledge base. Each skill is driven up a drill ladder (0 Orientation → 7 Transfer) until it reaches its target automaticity level (0–9); “losing” it is that level sliding back down. Decay strips the ladder top-down — under-pressure (level 7) erodes before under-time (5) before slow-but-correct (4) — which is why fluent speed and production always go first. The protocol is fixed: drive to the gate, drop daily drilling to a weekly review at target speed, and once it holds four weeks, to monthly — then let real use be the drill. But re-check every 4–6 weeks, because even substrate skills regress under pure neglect. Source: the wiki's early-childhood-skill-stack, automaticity-and-reflex-training, and spaced-repetition.

V   Two Doors

If not the UK, then which?

Both deliver the same prize — free state schooling for a resident child at a fraction of the British visa cost. They differ in what they ask of you first.

Bundesrepublik

Germany

Language

The real toll. Daily life and most schools run in German; English-only narrows your options fast.

Path to PR

Fastest in Europe — 21 months on a Blue Card with a B1 certificate, 27 without.

Schooling

Strong, free, well-funded public system. Early tracking into Gymnasium vs other paths.

Cost of living

Berlin still cheaper than Amsterdam or London; housing pressure rising but manageable.

Best if you'll learn German — and want permanent residence quickly.
Nederland

Netherlands

Language

English works almost everywhere. International streams and bilingual schools are common.

Path to PR

Five years of residence, plus the civic-integration (inburgering) exam for the permanent card.

Schooling

Free, high-ranking public system. A generous network of partly-subsidised international schools.

Cost of living

Higher — Amsterdam housing is the tightest in this comparison. Smaller cities ease it.

Best if you want a soft landing in English from day one.

VII   The Sequence

Four moves, in order.

This Month Step 00

Inventory your skills.

List what you and your partner do for work: industry, years of experience, languages, qualifications. This single sheet of paper decides which countries are reachable.

Next Two Weeks Step 01

Test the market.

Search visa-sponsorship listings on indeed.co.uk, make-it-in-germany.com, and iamexpat.nl/career. Whichever country surfaces the most realistic matches is the country your plan should target.

Within Two Months Step 02

Pick the Tbilisi bridge school.

Visit two or three Cambridge-aligned schools. Talk to parents at the pickup gate. Decide before the budget arrives so enrollment is administrative, not exploratory.

~12 Months Out Step 03

Enroll. Apply.

Child starts the Cambridge programme. Visa or job application continues in parallel — the two timelines don't have to converge.

When Visa Lands Step 04

Move. Transfer.

Cambridge-to-Cambridge in the UK, or Cambridge-to-free-state-school in Germany or the Netherlands. No lost year. No curriculum gap. The 12-year arc stays intact.

Rider Your partner's work — document translation across Georgian, Russian and English — isn't a local-sponsorship role anywhere, so it can't be the second key. But it is remote and portable: it travels intact to whichever country the first key opens.
North Landing safely is the floor, not the aim. This move is for something — to put you where your mind is fed the newest and most valuable ideas, and where the work you do can change things. That's a second compass, and it pulls the other way: the calmest country and the most ideas-rich one are seldom the same place.
The read.   Two needs, pulling apart. A calm life points at Ireland — English, EU, free state schools, a Critical-Skills route built for senior software, and a gentler, cheaper home. A mind at the frontier points at London — the one place that is genuinely ideas-dense and English and reachable on a sponsored visa, with the cost carried by the package. So: London if you're reaching, Ireland if you're nesting — and either is a good answer. The summit cities (think San Francisco) feed the mind most, but they'd eat the very evenings this whole plan exists to protect; that's a later door, not this one. Germany or the Netherlands only if someone will carry the German or Dutch — the one cost that competes with the time this plan protects.

Reference   The Terms

Jargon, decoded.

IHS
Immigration Health Surcharge — £1,035 per person per year, paid upfront for the whole UK visa term to use the NHS.
State school
UK government-funded, free school. ("Public school" in Britain confusingly means an elite private one.)
Catchment area
The zone a UK state school admits from. Where you live decides which school your child can attend.
Skilled Worker visa
The main UK work route. Needs a Home-Office-licensed sponsor and a salary above the threshold.
Blue Card
EU work-and-residence permit for graduates in shortage roles. Germany's fastest path to permanent residence.
B1
Intermediate level on the CEFR language scale. A German B1 certificate shortens the PR wait to 21 months.
HSM
Highly Skilled Migrant — the Netherlands' fast work route, gated by salary and a recognised sponsor.
IND
Immigratie- en Naturalisatiedienst — the Dutch immigration service. Sponsors must be IND-recognised.
Inburgering
The Dutch civic-integration requirement — a language and society exam on the road to permanent residence.
IGCSE / A-Level
Cambridge's exams at 16 and 18 — the portable certificates that transfer across the 160-country network.
